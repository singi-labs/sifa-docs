title: Connect a GitHub account
description: Link your GitHub account so your merged pull requests show up in your Sifa activity feed and contribution graph.

Connect your GitHub account to Sifa ID and your merged pull requests appear in your [activity feed](/docs/activity-feed) and contribution graph, alongside the rest of your Atmosphere activity.

Sifa reads **merged pull requests you authored** in public repositories. Not your repositories, not commits, not issues, not stars.

## Pick one route

There are two separate ways to connect a GitHub account. **Do one or the other, not both.** They are different entry points, not steps in a sequence:

- **Option 1: add it in Sifa, then link back from your GitHub profile** (recommended). You add the account under External accounts and put your Sifa profile link on your GitHub profile.
- **Option 2: verify it on [Keytrace](https://keytrace.dev)**. You do not add anything in Sifa. Keytrace verifies the account and Sifa surfaces it automatically.

Both prove you own the account, which is required before Sifa reads anything. We recommend Option 1: it is self-contained, just GitHub and your Sifa profile, with no other service to sign up for. Option 2 is a convenient shortcut, especially if you already use Keytrace or want to verify several accounts at once.

## Option 1: add it in Sifa, link back from GitHub

**Step 1: add the account.** Open your profile, go to **External accounts**, and add an account with:

- **Platform**: pick **GitHub**.
- **URL**: your profile address, for example `https://github.com/<your-username>`.

Save. Until you finish step 2 it shows as a link only, with nothing pulled in.

**Step 2: link back from your GitHub profile.** Put your Sifa profile URL (for example `https://sifa.id/p/<your-username>`) on your GitHub profile, in either of these places:

- **Website** field, or
- one of the four **Social accounts** slots

Both are under **Settings -> Public profile**. GitHub marks both with `rel="me"`, which is what Sifa looks for. Your DID works in place of the URL if you prefer.

A link in your profile **README** or bio text does not work. Sifa reads the structured profile links, not free text.

Once the link is in place, use the **Verify** button on the account in Sifa to check right away, or leave it and Sifa re-checks periodically.

## Option 2: verify on Keytrace

Verify your GitHub account on [Keytrace](https://keytrace.dev), which walks you through publishing a signed claim. That is the whole flow.

You do **not** add the account under External accounts for this route. Sifa detects the Keytrace claim and reads your pull requests on its own.

## What shows up

- **Merged pull requests you authored**, in public repositories. Open and closed-unmerged PRs are left out.
- Each card shows the repository, the title, the language, and the diff size.
- A run of merges with nothing in between is **grouped into one card** ("N merged pull requests") rather than filling your feed. Open it to see the individual PRs.
- Your merges also feed the **contribution graph** on your activity page.

## How far back, and how often

Sifa reads roughly **the last year** of merged pull requests, which is the window the contribution graph shows. Older history is not collected.

Timing:

- The first read happens when you verify.
- After that, Sifa refreshes when your profile is viewed, at most once a day.
- Filling in your full year runs as a background job and can take a few passes, so if your history is long, expect it to keep filling in for a while after the first merges appear.

## Not showing up?

- **Check the account is verified.** Unverified accounts show as a link only and are never read. The account should show a checkmark on your profile.
- **Check the link is in a profile field, not your README.** Website or Social accounts, under Settings -> Public profile.
- **Check the PRs are merged and public.** Open PRs, closed-unmerged PRs, and anything in a private repository are all excluded by design.
- **Give it a day.** If you just verified, the first read is immediate but the full history fills in behind it.
